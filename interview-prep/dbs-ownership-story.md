# DBS SRE automation → sole maintenance → Spark development

## Facts supplied by the candidate

- Worked on the DBS engagement through NCS for approximately three years.
- First year: five-person SRE automation team; candidate was a main developer and knew the application especially well.
- Second year: team was reduced until candidate alone maintained the application.
- Third year: maintenance workload had become light, and the manager assigned Spark data development in addition.
- Technologies already described in the CV: Kotlin, Spring Boot, WebFlux, Spark, Kafka, Hive, HDFS, YARN, S3, Jenkins, Helmfile, OpenShift and GCP.

## Interview positioning

This story demonstrates **ownership**, **operational reliability**, and **adaptability**. The strongest evidence is not the shared Singapore location of DBS and UOB, but sustained responsibility for a banking application across development and maintenance.

Avoid: “My manager saw I had nothing to do.”

Prefer: “As the SRE automation platform matured, its maintenance workload decreased, which gave me the capacity to take on Spark-based data development.”

## Likely follow-up questions

1. Which backend modules did you personally build in the first year?
2. How did the five-person team divide responsibilities?
3. Why did the team shrink? Was the project completed, or were resources reallocated?
4. What did being the sole maintainer actually involve?
5. What was the most difficult production incident or change request you handled alone?
6. How were releases, access controls, testing and change approvals handled?
7. How did you keep the application supportable with only one maintainer?
8. Which Spark job did you develop and what were its Kafka offset/checkpoint/recovery semantics?
9. What part of your work was hands-on Java/Kotlin coding rather than operations?
10. What would you improve if you redesigned the automation application?

Prepare answers from actual work. Do not imply a formal leadership title or invent throughput, incident counts, team restructuring motives, or outcomes.

## A 60-second DBS answer

> At DBS, my role evolved over three years. I started as one of the main developers in a five-person SRE automation team, building Kotlin and Spring-based tooling for the data platform. As the application matured and the team was reduced, I became its sole maintainer in the second year because I knew the implementation particularly well. By the third year, routine maintenance required less effort, so I also took on Spark data development involving Kafka-based batch processing. That experience gave me ownership across the application lifecycle, from development to long-term support, while broadening my skills in data engineering.
