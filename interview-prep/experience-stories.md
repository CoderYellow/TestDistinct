These prompts reflect the submitted CV and additional first-hand clarification. Do not invent performance metrics, ownership beyond what was stated, or confidential client details.

## DBS — the primary interview story (three-year progression)

**Year 1 — Core developer in a five-person SRE automation team.** Worked as a main developer of the Kotlin/Spring Boot/WebFlux automation tooling for the DBS data platform, becoming one of the people most familiar with its implementation.

**Year 2 — Sole maintainer.** As the team was reduced, became the only engineer maintaining the application because of deep familiarity with its design and codebase. Describe this as *sole maintainer* or *primary owner for ongoing maintenance*, not a formally appointed tech lead unless that title was actually held.

**Year 3 — Expanded into Spark data development.** Once the automation application had matured and routine maintenance work had declined, the manager assigned additional Spark-based data development responsibilities, including Kafka batch workflows.

**What this demonstrates:** end-to-end ownership, maintaining a system after its initial build, production support and adaptability across backend and data engineering.

### DBS spoken answer

> For the past three years, I've been working at NCS on projects for DBS Bank, primarily covering SRE automation and, more recently, Spark-based data development.
>
> My responsibilities have evolved quite significantly over these three years.
>
> During my first year, I worked on an SRE automation project as part of a five-person team. I was one of the main developers and became deeply familiar with the system, particularly its backend implementation and operational workflows.
>
> The project involved developing automation services using Kotlin, Spring Boot and WebFlux, integrating operational capabilities across the bank's big data platform.
>
> In my second year, as the team was gradually scaled down, I became the sole engineer responsible for maintaining the application.
>
> Because I had been heavily involved in its development, I was already familiar with its architecture and implementation, so I was able to take over the ongoing maintenance independently.
>
> This experience taught me a great deal about ownership. I wasn't just implementing individual features; I was responsible for keeping an existing system maintainable and supporting its day-to-day operation.
>
> By my third year, the automation platform had become relatively stable, and the maintenance workload had decreased.
>
> As a result, my manager gave me additional responsibilities in Spark-based data development, particularly working with Kafka data sources and batch processing.
>
> So, over these three years, I've had the opportunity to work across backend development, production support, automation and big data engineering.
>
> I think the most valuable part of this experience is that I've been able to take ownership of a system over its lifecycle, rather than simply moving from one development task to another.
>
> And because this work was for DBS, I've also gained practical experience working within a Singapore banking technology environment.
